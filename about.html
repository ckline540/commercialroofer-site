About

We are not consultants in suits.

We are builders, leaders, and operators who lived the grind and figured out how to scale past it. Everything we teach, we run in our own companies first.

The founder

Cody Kline

Out of the Air Force in 2012 and onto a roof at nine dollars an hour, carrying screws and running the lift. He figured out the sales guys made the money, learned the trade from the top of the ladder down, and started Jag Roofing and Sheet Metal. He sold it to private equity in 2021 and then grew their commercial division.

Today he and three partners own commercial and residential roofing companies across the country. His three commercial locations will do more than sixty million dollars this year. CommercialRoofer.com is where that playbook gets handed to everyone else.

What we believe

Recurring revenue for an old-school trade.

Most roofing companies wait on storms, referrals, general contractors, and bid lists for their commercial work. We think a commercial division should create its own opportunities every morning, walk roofs every week, and get paid every year on service agreements, not once on a replacement. That is the business we build with every company in the network.

Find the work yourself.

Ugly roof hunting puts the opportunity in your hands before anyone else knows it exists. No bid room, no ad budget, no waiting.

Sell the small things first.

The first visit is a repair ticket, not a replacement pitch. The service agreement comes next. The replacement comes to you when it comes.

Run the numbers out loud.

Every company in the network keeps a weekly scorecard. We look at them together on the live call. What gets measured gets walked.

How we work

Our language is confident, clear, and candid.

We do not use corporate buzzwords or speak in circles. We call things what they are, and we speak with respect, not ego. We say what we mean, we keep it simple, and we lead with purpose.

Be impeccable with your word
Always do your best
Do not make assumptions
Speak with sincere candor
Do not take anything personally
The network

Two hundred sixty companies. Forty-six states. One play.

Residential companies with commercial as a twinkle in their eye. Family companies that have been at it since the fifties. Startups with one truck. They all run the same play, keep the same scorecard, and show up to the same live call every week. A few times a year we get in a room together.
